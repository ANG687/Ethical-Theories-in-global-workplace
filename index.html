Theoretical Matrix & Comparative Framework

Strategic summary of theories and global workplace applications.

Comparative Matrix
Theory Main Focus Global Workplace Application Managerial Imperative
Deontology Duties, principles, rights Human rights, equality, compliance, anti-discrimination Non-negotiable moral duties overriding economic gain.
Utilitarianism Greatest overall benefit Employee wellbeing, sustainability, organisational decisions Cost-benefit calculation evaluating collective impact.
Stakeholder Theory Responsibilities to all stakeholders ESG, responsible management, global workforce considerations Long-term value creation across ecosystems.
Discourse Theory Fair and inclusive dialogue Employee voice, cultural inclusion, participative leadership Communicative rationality & psychological safety.
Key Takeaway: In global workplaces, these theories are often complementary rather than competing. Effective managers balance principles and rights (Deontology), outcomes and consequences (Utilitarianism), stakeholder interests (Stakeholder Theory), and inclusive participation and dialogue (Discourse Theory). Together, they support ethical leadership in culturally diverse and globally connected organisations.
